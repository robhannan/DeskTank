- T4b rigid bodies and glue (D17): a `rigid` movement class (rock); each pour or stroke of rock is one body (cells carry the body id in `state`, a `BTreeMap` body table in `World`) that sinks a row per tick through lighter fluids, or rises through heavier ones, as one piece, resting on powders, other bodies and the floor. Scooped bodies split into 4-connected pieces at the next tick. A body being poured into is held still until the stroke ends. A glue tool (palette slot 10, key 0 in `desktank window`) toggles bodies static; glued cells carry a variant bit the palette shades amber. - T4c rigid-body physics (D17 revised): bodies keep a fixed-point pose (position, velocity, angle, spin) and a reference shape they are redrawn from when the drawn pose changes, keeping their cell count. Gravity accelerates them (capped at 4 cells per tick); buoyancy and quadratic drag from the displaced fluid give terminal speeds (rock about 0.85 cells per tick in water, driftwood about 0.33 rising). A resting body whose centre of mass lies outside its contacts, or that sits on a single point, tips about the contact; bodies slide off a step, a fast body pushes sand and gravel aside (moved, never deleted) and embeds deeper than one set down gently, and a hit passes momentum to a free body. Bodies at rest sleep. Mechanisms in SPEC §2.1 *Rigid bodies*; code in `desktank_sim::world::bodies::{motion, shape}`. Tests in `crates/desktank-sim/tests/bodies.rs`.

- T6b plant individuals and species (D18, SPEC §2.7): every plant is an entry in a `BTreeMap` plant table (species, age, health, stored energy, part counts, base, bounds, parent, generation) whose id is in its cells' `state`; cells use the generic part materials root, stem and leaf, with the part-local position in the variant byte's low 6 bits. Species are TOML in `assets/plants/` (`desktank_sim::plants`): hygrophila (T6's plant, the default), java moss (T6's moss, replacing the `moss` material) and rotala (strong light), each with light/nutrient/CO₂ ranges, growth rate and size, hardiness, propagation and colour ramps. Random ticks sample the fields into each plant; a plant update (each plant every 5 s, ≤ 16 a tick) turns them into health and energy, grows cells from the species' form, drops the oldest leaves below half health and kills the plant at 0. Scooping splits a plant at the next tick into a fragment (parent id set) or dead plant matter, by species. The cell texture is `Rgba8Unorm` (material, variant, state) and a 256×256 plant texture holds each plant's health tint, so a starving plant yellows and browns without waking a chunk. One plants slot leads the Plants palette group and pours the picked species, replacing the seed, cutting and moss swatches #21 added (window: click it again or `P` cycles, the title names it; web: a picker beside the palette); inspect shows species, age, health, size and the limiting factor; `World::ecosystem` counts plant cells from the table; the cap is 2048 plants. - T8 persistence: `desktank_sim::save` (OS-agnostic, builds for wasm32 for WB3) encodes a `World` as a 20-byte envelope (magic `DeskTank`, format version, reserved, payload length, CRC-32) around an LZ4 block of a `postcard` snapshot (`save::v1`, frozen types; `decode` dispatches on the version, so v2 adds a module and a migration). The snapshot holds everything that feeds later ticks: size, seed, tick, surface row; the grid as planes (material and state run-length encoded, colour noise XOR the fresh-tank noise); each chunk's pending work, changed rect and sleep timer; the body table with pose and reference shape and the next body id; the random-tick lists in scheduler order, cursor and budget; the nutrient, CO₂ and light fields with their settling flags; the plant table with its update bookkeeping, next id, cursor and cap; the creatures and next id; and every material and species id's name, checked on load. RNG streams are derived from (seed, tick, stream), so none are stored. Loads validate sizes, indices, poses and finite values (`SaveError`). `desktank_platform::SaveStore` keeps saves as `main-NNNNNNNNNN.dtank` in `data_dir()/tanks` (`DESKTANK_DATA_DIR` overrides): writes go to `main.tmp`, fsync, rename to the next generation, fsync the directory, then prune to the newest 3; ownership is an OS advisory lock (`File::try_lock`) on `main.lock`; a save that doesn't load is renamed `.dtank.damaged` and the next older one is tried. `desktank window` opens the saved tank (at its saved size) or a new starter scene, autosaves every 5 minutes from its existing wake-ups (no timers), saves when hidden and on exit (`exiting`, which also runs on macOS Quit), and with the tank owned elsewhere shows the newest save read-only; `--fresh` and `--no-save`; `--demo-pour` never saves. Tests: `crates/desktank-sim/tests/save.rs` (round trip gives the same hash and identical re-encoded bytes; a loaded tank carries on tick for tick, checked through moving and tipping bodies; forks saved at many ticks of a pour; the `tests/fixtures/v1.dtank` fixture loads and runs; damaged bytes rejected), unit tests for the envelope and inconsistent snapshots, `SaveStore` unit tests, `crates/desktank-platform/tests/kill_mid_save.rs` (a child writing 8 MB saves is killed 10 times, a new owner always loads an intact save) and `persist` tests in `desktank` (damaged save moved aside for a fresh tank, a second process never writes).
- T6d (visual subset) plant appearance: plant cells are drawn from their species at two texels per cell side, like the sprites (D19), instead of one flat palette colour per cell. A species texture (32×256, one row per species id: each part's colour ramp resampled to 8 shades, then the part shapes and sizes) and the plant texture's alpha (now the plant's species id; RGB stays the health tint) feed the grid shader, which reads a plant cell's same-plant neighbours to orient and shape it. Shapes are data: `[look]` in the species TOML picks `leaf` (lanceolate, needle, round, feather), `stem` (stalk, thick; fuzz, frond for mats) and `root` (strand, thick). Shades vary by position: stems and leaves lighten (or, for rotala, redden) with height on the stem, leaf tops and tips are lighter, roots lighten towards the tip, mats fray and lighten where they face water, and the variant noise bits add a step either way. Empty texels show the neighbouring water or substrate. Hygrophila has broad lanceolate leaves, rotala fine needles going from green to red-orange up the stem, java moss a dark fuzzy mat. Below scale 2 shapes are filled. The palette no longer flags plant parts. Ramps grew to 3–6 shades, so the swatch colour (the middle leaf shade) changed, most visibly rotala's (now gold); a `[look]` stem shape must suit the form (stalk or thick for stems, fuzz or frond for mats). Plant texels with no plant carry species 0, so no species id is reserved. Tests: species texture layout and resampling, shader constants against the shape enums, `[look]` parsing; render references `planted_*` re-blessed and a new `plant_species` scene. Static detail: a settled tank still draws no frames.

Tests: each species' look, colours and care relative to the others and a distinct swatch per species (`plants.rs`); grown alone for 10 minutes, cabomba leads in open light and collapses under a lid (held back by light) while bacopa hardly notices, and christmas moss creeps over rock slower than java moss (`plant_individuals.rs`); the web picker lists all seven; the `plant_species` render scene now shows all seven side by side (re-blessed).
- Hotfix: the WebGPU web build rendered a black canvas in Chrome because Tint rejected `tank.wgsl` (`mixing '*' and '^' requires parenthesis` in `fn hash`); the operands are now parenthesised, and `crates/desktank-render/tests/wgsl_precedence.rs` scans every `.wgsl` in the crate for a bitwise operator (`&`, `|`, `^`) mixed with another binary operator at the same nesting level (single `<`/`>` are skipped, as they can be template brackets).

## Learnings

- naga is laxer than Tint, Chrome's WGSL compiler: it accepts `a * b ^ c`, which WGSL forbids (bitwise operands must be unary expressions). Native tests and the Playwright tests (WebGL2 on SwiftShader, translated to GLSL by naga) never parse WGSL in the browser, so a Tint-only error shows up only as a black canvas on WebGPU. Parenthesise every operand of `&`, `|` and `^` that is not a single term; the `wgsl_precedence` test enforces it without a GPU.
- Lock screens are restricted: macOS allows no custom lock screen and Windows only a picture, slideshow or Spotlight (no live rendering), so "lock screen" mode may mean the screensaver shown while locked.
- Reported, unverified: Windows 11 24H2 broke several wallpaper apps (Microsoft compatibility hold, [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/04/14/windows_11_24h2_hold/)); test wallpaper mode on 23H2/24H2/25H2.
- Reported, unverified: macOS `legacyScreenSaver` (Sonoma–Tahoe) never deallocates `ScreenSaverView`s ([cocoa-dev](https://lists.apple.com/archives/cocoa-dev/2024/Sep/msg00001.html)); see SPEC §3.4 for mitigations.
- GNOME's mutter has at most experimental wlr-layer-shell support, so Wayland wallpaper/overlay target wlroots compositors and KDE first.
- Cargo 1.100 warns on unused dependencies (`cargo::unused_dependencies`). Stub crates keep their SPEC §4.1 dependency edges with `use <crate> as _;`; drop those lines once the crate is really used.
- Dependabot labels 0.x bumps such as winit 0.30 → 0.31 as "minor", so the winit ignore covers semver-minor as well as major.
- Release profile uses `panic = "abort"` (smaller binaries, and a panic across the `.saver`'s `extern "C"` boundary aborts anyway). Revisit if a host ever needs `catch_unwind` to survive a panic.
- Stub release binaries on macOS: `desktank` 0.5 MB, `desktank-saver` 0.3 MB.
- winit 0.31 is still in beta (Nov 2026); use 0.30.
- T1 bench on the dev Mac (Apple Silicon, release): heavy pour 1.43 ms/tick early, 1.56 ms later (budget 5 ms); settled 4 µs (budget 0.5 ms). The pour settles after ~3100 ticks (155 s of world time).
- If two classes can each initiate the same exchange (water falling into a bubble, the bubble rising into water), the pair moves twice per tick and drifts sideways. Only liquids are displaceable; buoyant cells move themselves.
- A liquid that wanders sideways with no drop in reach never settles (chunks stay awake forever). Liquids only move towards a reachable drop, which leaves terraces of one cell per `LEVEL_REACH` columns.
- Diagonal-only powder slides give 45° piles; a per-row run pattern derived from `repose` gives any angle from 15° to 45° exactly.
- Falling-sand engines such as The Powder Toy appear (inferred, unmeasured) to process every live particle each frame; DeskTank must use sleeping chunks + dirty rects and a sim tick below the render rate to meet its budgets.

- wasm-bindgen's generated `unsafe` doesn't trip the workspace `unsafe_code = "deny"` lint (it comes from an external proc macro), so `desktank-web` needs no allow; CI runs clippy for `wasm32` to catch a change.
